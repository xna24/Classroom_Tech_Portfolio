## Activity 2 - Explanation

### Foot vs wheels

![Ancient footprints near Lake Mungo, NSW.](https://media.australian.museum/media/dd/images/Near_Lake_Mungo.width-1600.8e6265a.jpg){: style="display: block; width: 50%; height: auto; margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;" }

<p style="width: 50%; margin: 0.35rem auto 0; text-align: center; font-size: 0.75rem; color: #777;">
Ancient footprints near Lake Mungo, NSW. Image source: <a href="https://media.australian.museum/media/dd/images/Near_Lake_Mungo.width-1600.8e6265a.jpg">The Australian Museum</a>
</p>

It is no surprise people still walk with their own feet for the simple reasons that wheels cannot go up and down stairs, enter narrow spaces or climb slopes easily. A human foot is a marvellous system of bones, joints, tendons, ligaments and a product of millions of years of evolution. Crucially, unlike wheels, it is an integral part of our human body and does not need fitting, installing and specialised maintenance.

### Chisel vs pen

![The Martin Luther King, Jr. Memorial in Washington DC](https://www.nps.gov/mlkm/learn/images/3152655F-1DD8-B71C-075CAAAA42B532E7-large.jpg){: style="display: block; width: 50%; height: auto; margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;" }

<p style="width: 50%; margin: 0.35rem auto 0; text-align: center; font-size: 0.75rem; color: #777;">
The Martin Luther King, Jr. Memorial in Washington DC. Image source: <a href="https://www.nps.gov/mlkm/learn/images/3152655F-1DD8-B71C-075CAAAA42B532E7-large.jpg">National Park Service</a>
</p>

Chisel and stone are still used today even though paper, parchment and pen have been around for thousands of years. These are all technologies once used for communication, record-keeping, arts and story-telling. Chisel and stone are still used today and will still be around for the foreseeable future because there are still context calling for them: sculpturing, stone carvings on buildings and roads and headstones on graves. Some are called for due to the long durability of stone carvings, others for artistic expression where writing on paper (or printing on plates) cannot achieve the same effect or convey the same artistic expression (e.g., different depth of strokes in carvings).
