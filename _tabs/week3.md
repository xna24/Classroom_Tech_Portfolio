## Explanation

Australian Curriculum (v.9) general capability "Digital Literacy" contains 4 elements each with 3 sub-elements. The mind map above made with <a href="https://explaineverything.com/">Explain Everything</a> shows ideas about how these element might be realised in a secondary mathematics classroom. The ability to propose questions of investigation, gather, process, analyse data to gain insight and communicate findings is featured across several elements/subelements of digital literacy. One example is a collaborative investigation of correlation between lengths of forearm and height which is useful in clothing design was taken from [Bargagliotti et al. (2017, p. 89)](#gaise-ii).

Next, I will focus on the two subelements of the element "Practising digital safety and wellbeing". The first subelement, "manage digital privacy and identity" involves raising awareness of digital footprints and privacy online. The learning activity involves a hands-on experience of creating and brute-force hacking of ordinary passwords and could also include some basic knowledge of public key encryption for online communication. Through such activities, students will gains appreciation for strength of password and the importance of encrypted communication as a part of our modern digital infrastructure (e.g., HTTPS protocol). Guiding statement 1.2 in the Australian Framework for Generative Artificial Intelligence in Schools [(Australian Government, 2023)](#australian-framework) states that schools should educate students on the "potential limitation and biases" of generative AI tools. This is also also related to "AI-credibility", one of the three core concepts proposed by [Wyatt-Smith & Kimber (2024)](#wyatt-smith-2024) to build students' knowledge and skills in interacting with AI. A potential mathematics lesson starter involves a custom-build calculator tool that "hallucinates" at a prescribed rate which students must utilise to finish some computation for authentic tasks (such as calculating the amount of paint needed to paint a house or a room with given geometry). Students will learn to critically evaluate the answer instead of taking them at face value. They will be encouraged to collaborate, cross-check, discuss and use common-sense to estimate the accuracy of the answers. This simulates the experience of the use of generative AI tools (such as ChatGPT) where hallucination is an inherent flaw. It is worth noting that the lack of guardrails and wide-spread use of generative AI tools, along with its potential to hallucinate and spread misinformation are some of the reasons for placing "managing risks" at top of list of priorities in a survey of senior education leaders in Australia ([Bower et al. (2025)](#bower-et-al-2025)).
