# Portfolio Tasks (Option A): Annotated Digital Toolkit Table

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Why are there 3 tables instead of 1?</summary>

Digital tools/resources (in the most general definition) in the classroom can vary in terms of:
- range of possible use cases and creative freedom
- who the tool mainly targets (teachers or students)
- being a collection/library or a single applet

It is not clear to me which of these categories the phrase `select digital tools` refers to, so I decided to make a few tables targetting different categories.

In addition, it seems to also be reasonable to distinguish between:
- technology-enhanced learning for subject-specific outcomes
- building digital literacy of students in my teaching subjects (Mathematics and Science)
- building digital competencies for teachers

By the first item in the above list, I'm trying to say that not every learning activities using digital tool builds digital literacy in a meaningful way. For instance, a student could be using <a href="https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/pendulum-lab">this PhET pendulum simulation</a> to learn about periodic motion without also developing, e.g., ability to "collect and interpret data", to "create, communicate, collaborate", to "manage and protect content", or to "manage online safety" [ACARA (n.d.-a)](/Classroom_Tech_Portfolio/references/#acara-DL)

[Falloon (2022)](/Classroom_Tech_Portfolio/references/#falloon-2020)'s TDC framework focuses on **teachers'** (that is, the T in TDC) digital competence. In my opinion, a selection of a digital tool (e.g., Scootle, Wolfram Alpha) does not in itself "build" teacher's digital competencies. This is as oversimplifying as saying that listing ACARA's Australian Curriculum website enhances my "curriculum competency". I will therefore use TDC dimensions to signal corresponding elements of digital literacy for **students**. 
</details>
